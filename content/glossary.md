# The AI Sanity Plan — AI Glossary

> **AI moves fast. Terms, tools, and capabilities referenced here are accurate as of early 2026 but may evolve. When in doubt, check the source.**

---

## A Plain-Language Guide to the AI Terms You'll Actually Encounter

Look, I spent 40 years in business before AI became dinner conversation. The first time someone asked me about "transformer architecture" and "retrieval augmented generation," I smiled politely and quietly Googled both under the table. 

You shouldn't have to do that. This glossary covers the AI terms you're most likely to run into — as a business owner, not an engineer. Every definition is in plain language. No jargon explained with more jargon. If I catch myself writing something that sounds like a computer science textbook, I've failed. So let's get to it.

---

## A

### AGI (Artificial General Intelligence)
A hypothetical future AI that could think, reason, and learn across any subject the way a human can. We don't have it yet — not even close. What we have today are tools that are very good at specific tasks. Anyone telling you AGI is "right around the corner" is selling something.

> **🗣️ AI Ron Says:** "AGI is the AI version of flying cars. Everyone keeps promising it's five years away, and it's been five years away for decades. Don't plan your business around it. Plan around what works today."

### Agent (AI Agent)
An AI system that can take multiple steps on its own to complete a task — not just answer a question, but actually *do things* like browse the web, send emails, or fill out forms. Think of it as the difference between asking someone for directions and hiring someone to actually drive you there. Still early, and you'll want to supervise closely.

### Algorithm
A set of rules or instructions that tells a computer how to solve a problem or complete a task. Every AI tool runs on algorithms. You don't need to understand how they work any more than you need to understand how your car engine works — but it helps to know the word when someone throws it around in a meeting.

### API (Application Programming Interface)
A way for two pieces of software to talk to each other. When your scheduling app automatically pulls data from your AI tool, that's happening through an API. You'll never need to build one, but you'll hear the term constantly. Think of it as a universal translator between apps.

> **🗣️ AI Ron Says:** "When a vendor says 'we have an API,' what they mean is: their tool can connect to other tools. That's it. Don't let the acronym intimidate you."

### Artificial Intelligence (AI)
The broad term for computer systems that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence — like understanding language, recognizing patterns, or making predictions. In practical terms for your business, AI means tools like ChatGPT and Claude that can draft, summarize, analyze, and organize information at speed.

### Automation
Using technology to perform tasks with minimal human involvement. AI-powered automation goes beyond simple "if this, then that" rules — it can handle tasks that require judgment calls about language, tone, or context. Think auto-sorting your emails by priority or generating first drafts of weekly reports.

---

## B

### Benchmark
A standardized test used to measure how well an AI model performs compared to other models. When you see headlines like "Model X beats Model Y," they're talking about benchmark scores. Useful for comparing tools, but real-world performance in *your* business is what actually matters.

### Bias (AI Bias)
When an AI system produces unfair or skewed results because of problems in the data it was trained on. If the training data mostly contains examples from one demographic, industry, or perspective, the AI's outputs will lean that direction. It's not malicious — it's a math problem with real-world consequences.

> **🗣️ AI Ron Says:** "AI bias isn't some abstract ethics debate. If you ask AI to write a job description and it subtly skews toward one type of candidate because of its training data, that's a real legal and business risk. Always review what it gives you."

---

## C

### Chain-of-Thought (CoT)
A prompting technique where you ask the AI to show its reasoning step by step before giving a final answer. Instead of "What's the answer?" you say "Walk me through your thinking." This produces more accurate, reliable results — especially for anything involving numbers, logic, or complex decisions.

### Chatbot
Any software that communicates with you through text conversation. The chatbots of five years ago were frustrating, scripted disasters ("I'm sorry, I didn't understand that. Please choose from the following options..."). Modern AI chatbots like ChatGPT and Claude are dramatically better — they understand natural language and can hold genuinely useful conversations.

### ChatGPT
OpenAI's flagship AI assistant. It's the tool that kicked off the current AI wave when it launched in late 2022. Available in free and paid tiers. As of early 2026, the paid versions (Plus and Pro) offer more powerful models, image generation, file analysis, and web browsing.

### Claude
Anthropic's AI assistant and a direct competitor to ChatGPT. Known for being strong at long-form writing, nuanced analysis, and following complex instructions. Many business users (myself included) use both ChatGPT and Claude, since each has different strengths.

### Cloud AI
AI tools and models that run on remote servers you access through the internet, rather than on your own computer. ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini are all cloud AI. The advantage is power without hardware. The trade-off is that your data travels to someone else's servers.

### Context Window
The amount of text an AI can "see" and work with at one time — both your input and its output combined. Think of it as the AI's short-term memory. A small context window means the AI forgets earlier parts of a long conversation. Modern models have gotten much better at this, but it still has limits.

> **🗣️ AI Ron Says:** "If you've ever had AI seem to 'forget' what you told it earlier in a conversation, you probably ran into the context window limit. It's not being rude. It literally can't see that part of the conversation anymore."

### Copilot
Microsoft's AI assistant, built into Microsoft 365 products (Word, Excel, Outlook, Teams). If your business runs on Microsoft, this is the AI that shows up inside the tools you already use. The name is a good metaphor — it assists, it doesn't fly the plane.

### Custom GPT
A specialized version of ChatGPT that you can create for a specific purpose — like a customer service bot trained on your company's FAQs, or a writing assistant preloaded with your brand guidelines. No coding required. You just give it instructions and reference materials.

---

## D

### DALL-E
OpenAI's image generation tool, built into ChatGPT. You describe an image in plain language, and it creates one. Useful for quick marketing visuals, social media content, or brainstorming design concepts. Not a replacement for a professional designer, but a surprisingly capable starting point.

### Deep Learning
A subset of machine learning that uses neural networks with many layers to analyze complex patterns. It's the technology behind most modern AI breakthroughs — image recognition, language understanding, voice assistants. You don't need to understand how it works. Just know it's the engine under the hood.

### Diffusion Model
The technology behind most AI image generators (DALL-E, Midjourney, Stable Diffusion). It works by starting with random noise and gradually refining it into a coherent image based on your text description. It's basically sculpting a photo out of static.

---

## E

### Embedding
A way of converting text (or images, or audio) into numbers so AI can understand relationships between concepts. "Coffee" and "espresso" would have similar embeddings because they're related. This is the behind-the-scenes magic that lets AI understand that your question about "cutting costs" is related to "budget reduction" even though the words are different.

---

## F

### Few-Shot Prompting
Giving the AI a few examples of what you want before asking it to produce something. Instead of just saying "Write me a product description," you show it two or three examples of descriptions you like, then ask for a new one. Works remarkably well. One of the easiest ways to improve your results.

### Fine-Tuning
Taking an existing AI model and training it further on your own specific data so it becomes better at a particular task. Like hiring a smart generalist and giving them specialized training in your industry. Most business owners won't do this themselves, but you might use products that are built on fine-tuned models.

### Foundation Model
A large AI model trained on massive amounts of general data that serves as the base for many different applications. GPT-4, Claude, and Gemini are all foundation models. They're called "foundation" because they're the starting point — other tools and products are built on top of them.

---

## G

### Gemini
Google's AI model and assistant (formerly called Bard). Integrated into Google's ecosystem — Search, Workspace, Android. If you're a Google Workspace user, this is the AI that's showing up in your Gmail, Docs, and Sheets.

### Generative AI
AI that creates new content — text, images, audio, video, code — rather than just analyzing existing content. ChatGPT writing an email is generative AI. So is DALL-E creating an image or an AI tool composing music. This is the category of AI that's changed everything for business owners in the last few years.

> **🗣️ AI Ron Says:** "Most AI you used before 2023 was analytical — it sorted, predicted, and classified. Generative AI actually *makes things*. That's the shift. Your calculator could crunch numbers. Now your computer can write the memo that explains those numbers."

### GPT (Generative Pre-trained Transformer)
The specific type of AI model that powers ChatGPT. "Generative" means it creates content. "Pre-trained" means it learned from massive data before you ever talked to it. "Transformer" is the underlying architecture. When people say "GPT-4" or "GPT-4o," they're referring to specific versions of this model, each more capable than the last.

### Grounding
Connecting AI's responses to verified, factual sources rather than letting it generate answers purely from its training data. Grounded AI cites its sources. Ungrounded AI confidently makes things up. This is why tools like Perplexity (which searches the web in real-time) can be more reliable for factual questions.

### Guardrails
Built-in safety measures that prevent AI from generating harmful, inappropriate, or dangerous content. Every major AI tool has them. They're the reason ChatGPT won't write you instructions for anything illegal. Sometimes they're overly cautious — but on balance, you want them there.

---

## H

### Hallucination
When AI generates information that sounds completely plausible but is factually wrong — made-up statistics, fake citations, invented case studies, or nonexistent company names. This is the single most important limitation for business owners to understand. AI doesn't know it's wrong. It delivers nonsense with the same confidence as truth.

> **🗣️ AI Ron Says:** "Hallucinations are why I repeat this until I'm blue in the face: AI drafts, you decide. I've seen it invent court cases, fabricate financial data, and create entire fake studies — all with perfect formatting and total confidence. *Always verify.*"

---

## I

### Image Generation
AI's ability to create original images from text descriptions. Tools include DALL-E (inside ChatGPT), Midjourney, and Stable Diffusion. Quality has improved dramatically. Useful for social media, presentations, and concept mockups — though it still struggles with text in images and very specific details.

### Inference
When a trained AI model processes your input and generates a response. Every time you type a prompt and get an answer, that's inference. The term matters mainly because inference costs money (it takes computing power), which is why advanced AI tools charge for heavy usage.

---

## L

### Large Language Model (LLM)
The technology behind ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and similar tools. An LLM is trained on enormous amounts of text data and learns to predict the next word in a sequence. Call it the world's most sophisticated autocomplete. It doesn't "understand" language the way you do — it recognizes and reproduces patterns at an extraordinary scale.

> **🗣️ AI Ron Says:** "When someone says 'LLM,' just think 'the engine inside ChatGPT and Claude.' You'll be right 95% of the time, and that's close enough for every business decision you'll ever make."

### Latency
The delay between when you send a prompt and when you get a response. Lower latency means faster answers. You'll notice latency differences between models — the most powerful ones sometimes take longer because they're doing more work. For real-time applications like customer chat, latency matters a lot.

---

## M

### Machine Learning (ML)
A branch of AI where systems learn from data instead of following explicit programming. Instead of a programmer writing rules for every scenario, the system finds patterns on its own. Machine learning powers everything from your email spam filter to Netflix recommendations. It's the broad category that includes deep learning and LLMs.

### MCP (Model Context Protocol)
An open standard developed by Anthropic that lets AI assistants connect directly to your tools, databases, and business systems. Think of it as giving AI a set of universal plugs so it can talk to your CRM, calendar, file storage, or other software — without custom integration work for each one. Still emerging, but it's worth watching.

### Midjourney
A popular AI image generation tool known for producing highly artistic, stylized images. Accessed through Discord (which is a minor hassle). Many designers and marketers use it alongside DALL-E. Each tool has a slightly different aesthetic — Midjourney tends toward the more visually striking.

### Model
In AI, a "model" is the trained system that processes your inputs and generates outputs. When people say "GPT-4o" or "Claude 3.5 Sonnet," they're naming specific models. Think of models like car engines — they vary in power, speed, and efficiency. The model you use determines the quality and capability of the AI's responses.

### Multi-Modal
An AI that can work with more than just text — it can understand and generate images, audio, video, or combinations of all of them. Modern models like GPT-4o and Gemini are multi-modal. You can show them a photo and ask questions about it, or give them text and get an image back.

---

## N

### Natural Language Processing (NLP)
The field of AI focused on helping computers understand, interpret, and generate human language. It's why you can type a question in plain English (not code) and get a useful answer. NLP is what makes the entire "just talk to it like a person" experience possible.

### Neural Network
A computing system loosely inspired by the human brain, made up of layers of connected nodes that process information. It's the underlying structure of most modern AI. You don't need to understand how neural networks work — just know that when someone mentions them, they're talking about the architecture that makes AI possible.

---

## O

### Open Source
AI models and tools whose code is publicly available for anyone to use, modify, and build upon. Meta's LLaMA and Mistral are examples. Open-source models can be run on your own servers (which means your data stays private), but they generally require more technical setup. For most business owners, the commercial tools (ChatGPT, Claude) are more practical.

### OpenAI
The company that created ChatGPT and GPT models. Founded as a nonprofit in 2015, now operating as a capped-profit company. They're the 800-pound gorilla of the current AI landscape. Not the only game in town — Anthropic, Google, and Meta are all serious competitors — but they set the pace.

---

## P

### Parameters
The internal settings an AI model learned during training — think of them as the model's "knowledge connections." When you hear "a model with 70 billion parameters," that refers to the number of these connections. More parameters generally means more capability, but also more computing power required. You'll never adjust these. It's just a way to understand model size.

### Perplexity
An AI-powered search tool that combines the conversational style of ChatGPT with real-time web search and source citations. Unlike ChatGPT and Claude (which draw from training data), Perplexity actively searches the internet and tells you where its answers came from. Excellent for research and fact-checking.

### Prompt
The text you type into an AI tool to tell it what you want. Your prompt is your instruction, your question, your creative brief — all in one. The quality of your prompt directly determines the quality of the response you get back. Garbage in, garbage out. Precision in, precision out.

### Prompt Engineering
The practice of writing better prompts to get better AI outputs. It sounds fancier than it is. It's really about being specific, giving context, and showing the AI what good looks like. You don't need a certification. You need practice and the willingness to iterate.

> **🗣️ AI Ron Says:** "I hate the term 'prompt engineering.' It makes a simple concept sound like it requires a degree. Here's the whole secret: be specific, give context, and if you don't like what you get, ask again differently. That's it. You've been 'prompt engineering' every time you've given clear instructions to an employee."

---

## R

### RAG (Retrieval Augmented Generation)
A technique where AI pulls in specific, up-to-date information from external sources (like your company documents or a database) before generating a response. Instead of relying only on what it was trained on, the AI retrieves relevant facts first, then crafts its answer. This dramatically reduces hallucinations and makes AI responses more accurate and relevant.

> **🗣️ AI Ron Says:** "RAG is the difference between asking someone a question from memory versus asking someone who can check their notes first. You want the one who checks their notes."

### Reasoning Model
An AI model specifically designed to think through complex problems step by step before responding, rather than just generating the most likely next word. OpenAI's o1 and o3 models are examples. They're slower but significantly better at math, logic, coding, and multi-step analysis. Think of regular AI as answering off the top of its head, and reasoning models as sitting down to work through the problem.

### Reinforcement Learning
A training method where AI learns by receiving rewards for good outputs and penalties for bad ones. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) is how models like ChatGPT are refined after initial training — human reviewers rate the AI's responses, and it adjusts accordingly. It's basically the same way you train a golden retriever, but with more math.

---

## S

### SaaS (Software as a Service)
Software you access online through a subscription rather than installing on your computer. Nearly every AI tool you'll use is SaaS — you pay monthly, access it through a browser, and the company handles all the updates and infrastructure. ChatGPT Plus, Claude Pro, and Perplexity Pro are all SaaS products.

### Sentiment Analysis
AI's ability to determine the emotional tone of text — positive, negative, neutral, frustrated, excited, etc. Useful for monitoring customer reviews, survey responses, and social media mentions at scale. Instead of reading 500 customer comments, AI can summarize the overall sentiment in seconds.

### Stable Diffusion
An open-source AI image generation model. Unlike DALL-E and Midjourney, you can download and run Stable Diffusion on your own computer, which gives you more control and privacy. More technical to set up, but popular with businesses that need image generation without sending data to external servers.

### System Prompt
Hidden instructions given to an AI before your conversation begins that shape how it behaves. When you create a Custom GPT and tell it "You are a customer service representative for a plumbing company — always be helpful and refer complex issues to a human," that's a system prompt. It sets the AI's personality, constraints, and focus.

---

## T

### Temperature
A setting that controls how creative or predictable an AI's responses are. Low temperature (closer to 0) produces more consistent, focused, "safe" answers. High temperature (closer to 1 or 2) produces more creative, varied, sometimes surprising responses. For business writing, lower is usually better. For brainstorming, turn it up.

> **🗣️ AI Ron Says:** "Think of temperature like the 'adventurousness' dial on your AI. Low temperature is your accountant — precise, reliable, no surprises. High temperature is your marketing intern after three espressos — creative, unpredictable, occasionally brilliant."

### Token
The basic unit AI uses to process text. Roughly speaking, one token is about three-quarters of a word. "ChatGPT" is two tokens. "I love my business" is about five tokens. Tokens matter because AI tools have limits on how many tokens they can process at once (that's the context window), and pricing is often based on tokens used.

### Training Data
The massive collection of text, images, and other content used to teach an AI model. GPT-4 was trained on enormous amounts of internet text, books, articles, and code. The quality and breadth of training data directly affects what the AI knows and how well it performs. Training data has a cutoff date — which is why AI can be outdated on recent events.

### Transformer
The neural network architecture that powers virtually every modern LLM. Introduced by Google in 2017, it's the "T" in "GPT." Transformers are exceptionally good at understanding relationships between words across long passages of text. You don't need to know how they work — just know that this invention is the technical breakthrough that made all of today's AI tools possible.

---

## V

### Vector Database
A specialized database that stores information as mathematical representations (vectors) so AI can quickly find semantically similar content. This is what powers RAG systems — when you upload your company documents and want AI to search through them intelligently, a vector database is usually doing the heavy lifting behind the scenes.

### Voice AI
AI systems that can understand spoken language and respond with natural-sounding speech. This includes voice assistants, AI-powered phone systems, meeting transcription tools, and real-time translation. The quality has improved to the point where you can have a phone conversation with an AI and it genuinely sounds human.

---

## W

### Workflow Automation
Using AI to automate multi-step business processes that previously required manual work at each stage. Example: an email arrives, AI categorizes it, drafts a response, updates your CRM, and flags anything that needs your personal attention — all automatically. Tools like Zapier, Make, and n8n combined with AI make this increasingly accessible.

---

## Z

### Zero-Shot Prompting
Asking AI to perform a task without giving it any examples first — just a straight instruction. "Write me a product description for a leather wallet" is zero-shot. It often works fine for simple tasks, but for anything nuanced or brand-specific, few-shot prompting (giving examples first) tends to produce better results.
